# Story Bible: Feeding Hour

## Style guide

- **Voice:** Close third person, past tense. One point of view per chapter.
- **Tone:** Tense, grounded, and merciless. Dread first, spectacle second. Dry humor from people under stress is welcome, but it never softens the stakes. Nobody is safe because we like them.
- **Prose:** Concrete and physical. Name the sound, the smell, the weight. Avoid long stretches of explanation during action.
- **Scientific accuracy:** Keep the dinosaurs as close to current science as the story allows. That is why there is no Velociraptor pack. Theropods are feathered. Nothing hunts in cooperative packs unless the evidence supports it. Spinosaurus is a semi-aquatic fish-hunter with a paddle tail and is awkward on land. No dinosaur roars like a movie lion.
- **Sound is felt before it is heard.** Large dinosaurs, like elephants and crocodilians, communicate in low and infrasonic frequencies, with closed-mouth booms and rumbles. Characters feel it in the breastbone, teeth, floor, and water first, and the audible sound arrives a half-second behind. Animals sense it before people do. Use this for every large animal.
- **Dinosaurs as animals:** No monsters. They behave like animals: hungry, frightened, territorial, and confused by being in the wrong place and time. Theropods are feathered where the science supports it. No cinematic roaring on demand.
- **Animal chapters:** Close third on the animal, with no dialogue and no human thoughts. The animal perceives through scent, sound, vibration, and memory. No anthropomorphic speeches.
- **Chapter device:** Every chapter opens with an italic line giving the day and time ("*Saturday, 1:12 p.m.*"). The book runs from early afternoon to the following dawn.
- **Chapter length:** At least 2,000 words, aiming for 2,200 to 2,600 from here on (chapters 1 to 3 ran long).
- **Violence:** Graphic when it happens, never gratuitous. Consequences stay on the page. Deaths are final and are not undone by last-second rescues.
- **Scene breaks:** `* * *` on its own line.

## Setting

**Calder Bay Zoo** is a mid-sized zoo in the fictional port city of Calder Bay. It is built on a former rail yard and a hill of old quarry fill. About 2,000 visitors are inside on the Saturday of the Fall Festival. A ring road of service tunnels, called **the Ring** by staff, runs under the site, linking the night houses of the main exhibits. Zoo staff believe the Ring is for utilities and animal transfers.

**Zones:**

- **Main Gate and Carousel Court:** the central plaza, with the carousel, food court, and education center.
- **African Plains:** a large open yard with a dry moat, a lion kopje (rock pile), and an elephant barn.
- **Okavango Lagoon:** a big water exhibit with a bloat of five hippos and eight Nile crocodiles, a boat ride (Hippo Harbor Cruises), a pier, and a viewing deck. The crocodiles are solitary and territorial and each holds its own stretch of shore. They do not cooperate with each other or with the hippos.
- **Taiga Trail:** wooded Amur tiger exhibit with a glass tunnel that runs through it, a glass roof, and a keepers' building on the north side. A green steel door in the yard wall, half hidden in the ferns, is the old pump-room door and leads straight to the Ring. It is separate from the night house, where Katya's cubs are shut in.
- **Giraffe Overlook:** a tall feeding platform and a large paddock next to the Plains.
- **Arctic Coast:** polar bear exhibit with a deep pool. Background only.
- **The Reptile House:** closed for two years for "renovation". It sits above the lab's service lift and has the new rooftop cooling plant.

**The old rail yard.** Under the elephant barn is the filled-in pit of an old roundhouse **turntable**. A service cut, the **rail cut**, runs from the pit under the hill and out to the old harbor spur beyond the zoo fence. It is not on the zoo's maps. Only Gerry Ames knows it well. It is the one way out of the sealed zoo.

## The lab

**Meridian Biologics** leases the sublevels from the zoo's board through a shell conservancy. Cover story: a "genetic rescue" program for endangered species. In truth, **Project Halcyon** has been reviving extinct animals from preserved proteins and genomic reconstruction.

- **Sublevel 1:** Reception, labs, offices, and the lounge where the investors are held.
- **Sublevel 2:** Incubation and nursery.
- **Sublevel 3:** Large paddocks and the control room, about 60 feet below the zoo. The cooling plant is on the Reptile House roof.
- **The Ring access:** Sealed hatches connect the paddocks to the zoo's Ring. Several were never fully blocked from the zoo side.
- **Sublevel 5:** Not on any floor plan the staff received. Its lift key is held by Warren Teague alone. It holds **cryogenic vaults with Smilodon embryos** (Phase Three, Pleistocene), which only Teague and a few others know about.

**The program's mandate** is the Cretaceous. "Cretaceous" spans about eighty million years, so the staff joke constantly that Halcyon is a zoo of animals that never met each other.

**The feed tone.** Every animal in the lab has been fed to a tone sounded in each paddock at 18:00 every day since hatching. It is a low two-note bell with an infrasonic layer under it that the animals feel in their bodies, broadcast on the paddock and Ring speakers, on its own battery backup, and it is in Bao's schedule on the control-room wall. The animals are conditioned to come to it. Teague plans to sound it to recall them. Calloway plans to use the same tone to draw every dinosaur to one place.

**Ash Protocol.** Meridian's site-denial plan, written by Teague to destroy evidence if the program were ever exposed. Demolition charges are set in the Ring and under Paddock Three and the Reptile House. Teague has the abort code. The charges can bring the hill down on the sublevels and the Ring.

## The breach

1. A new rooftop cooling plant went online two weeks ago to impress an investor. It draws on the same power loop (the B loop) as the paddock door controllers, and that loop shares a feeder with the zoo's north hill (Taiga Trail and the Reptile House).
2. On the investor demonstration day (a Saturday, chosen because crowds help hide traffic), the cooling plant trips and sheds load at 1:51 p.m. Door controllers revert to their **fail-open default** because Meridian's safety engineer (Bao Tran) was overruled when he asked for fail-closed (Request for Change 114).
3. Four dinosaurs get out: Duchess, Spino, Jazz, and Atlas. Others remain in sublevel paddocks or hatchery rooms.
4. Warren Teague orders a **site-seal** at 1:53 p.m.: the lab's exterior doors and the zoo gates' remote locks are closed, the exterior jammers go up (killing cell service across the zoo), and no alert goes to the zoo. Calloway defies Teague and goes up the east stair with Imogen.
5. The zoo hears the truth at about 2:40 p.m., when Calloway and Bao patch the lab's paging system into the keeper radio channel and broadcast what is loose, where it came from, and that the gates are locked on purpose (Chapter 10).

## The animals

### Dinosaurs (project designations)

| Designation | Species | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **R-1 ("Duchess")** | *Tyrannosaurus rex* | About 40 ft, 8 tons. Raised in isolation. Big, hungry, wary, and quiet on her feet. Female. Silent and methodical rather than roaring. |
| **S-1 ("Barge")** | *Spinosaurus aegyptiacus* | About 45 ft, semi-aquatic hunter. Drawn to water. Enters the lagoon through the drain culvert. |
| **U-2 ("Jazz")** | *Utahraptor ostrommaysorum* | About 20 ft, about 1,000 lb, feathered. A solitary ambush hunter, smart and unpredictable. Named Jazz because she never hit the same note twice. Female. Killed a technician in March, which Meridian covered up. |
| **J-1 ("Atlas")** | *Brontosaurus excelsus* | A runt adult, about 20 ft and under 2 tons. Late Jurassic, not Cretaceous. The program's first success, kept as a pet. Gentle but easily spooked. **The only Jurassic specimen: the original sample was consumed in making him.** |

### Zoo animals

- **Imani:** African elephant matriarch, 41, about 6 tons. Calm, protective, deeply bonded to Mags.
- **Zola:** Imani's daughter, 22. Big and uncertain, protective of her calf.
- **Pip:** Zola's calf, 4. Curious and small enough to be in real danger.
- **Zuberi:** Male African lion, 9. Scarred, tired, and vigilant.
- **Amara and Sabi:** Lionesses who do most of the hunting.
- **Kito:** Zuberi's 3-year-old son, impulsive.
- **Katya:** Amur tigress, 14. Cautious, deadly, and a mother.
- **Misha and Dasha:** Katya's yearling cubs.
- **The hippo bloat (five):** **Gideon**, the bull, 3 tons, territorial and huge; the cows **Hattie**, **Mabel**, and **Pearl**; and **Biscuit**, a 3-year-old subadult. They act as one group, with a bite of about 1,800 psi and tusks.
- **The crocodiles (eight):** Solitary Nile crocodiles, each guarding its own stretch of shore. The largest is **Old Nile**, about 15 ft. They avoid each other and flee or posture when threatened. They never ambush as a team.
- **Kofi:** Male reticulated giraffe, 5 years old, 2,800 lb. High-strung, kicks first.
- **Nanuq:** Male polar bear, 11, about 1,300 lb. Background only.

## People

### Priya Raman, 17
Senior in high school, aiming at veterinary school. Practical and careful, a planner who hates not having a plan. Took her brother to the zoo for his birthday with money from babysitting. Their mother works double shifts at the hospital. Priya carries the weight of being the "responsible one". **Likely survivor.**

### Arjun Raman, 9
Obsessed with dinosaurs. Corrects people constantly ("birds *are* dinosaurs"). Brave in a way that is both a gift and a danger. Knows more about the animals than the adults think. **Guaranteed survivor.**

### Eddie Marsh, 26
Carnivore keeper on Taiga Trail. Two years on the job. Kind, nervous, overly competent in a crisis once he accepts it is one. Dies protecting the Ramans.

### Mags Delacroix, 58
Elephant keeper, 30 years at Calder Bay Zoo. Quiet, stubborn, funny in a dry way. Her husband Leon died four years ago, and Imani is the closest thing she has to family. Has a bad left knee.

### Gerry Ames, 73
Volunteer, retired railroad signalman. Narrow, quiet, and easy to overlook, with a steel tally clicker he uses constantly. He knows the old rail yard the zoo is built on, including the roundhouse turntable pit and the rail cut. A stealth character who matters more than anyone notices.

### Carla, educator
Green-uniformed zoo educator who gives talks on the Plains. Cheerful, steady with groups.

### Bev, Ops dispatcher
Runs the zoo's operations desk and the keeper radio channels.

### Dr. Tamsin Ode, 39
Zoo veterinarian. Fast, unsentimental, loyal to the animals. Carries a dart rifle.

### Theo Lindqvist, 34
Pilot of the Okavango boat ride. Ex-merchant mariner. Lost his license over a drinking incident and has been sober for three years. Quiet authority on the water.

### Rosalind Pike, 49
Off-duty paramedic on Theo's boat with her teenage daughter **Jess, 15**. Triage is her native language.

### Dr. Imogen Vale, 46
Project lead of Halcyon. Brilliant, tired, and complicit. She raised the safety flag and then signed off anyway. She goes after Atlas because he is the only Jurassic animal, and with him dies the chance of ever making another. **Likely survivor.** She faces the book's hardest choice.

### Warren Teague, 52
Vice president of operations at Meridian. Smooth, calm, and unwavering. He believes the project is worth any risk and sincerely thinks the crowd upstairs is manageable. Holds the only Sublevel 5 key and the Ash Protocol abort code.

### Dale Calloway, 44
Meridian head of security. Ex-military. Follows orders until the orders are plainly wrong. Breaks with Teague and fights him for control of the control room and the feed tone.

### Bao Tran, 31
Safety engineer. Wrote Request for Change 114 (fail-closed doors) four times. Scared of the building and the first to say so. Stays with Calloway as the person who can make the systems do what they need.

### Ruiz
Meridian deputy security chief. Loyal to Teague and the chain of command. Guards the control room.

### Hugh Ashworth, 61
Zoo director. Knows nothing about the lab. Learns too late.

### Carrow and Whitcomb
Investors held in the Sublevel One lounge by the site-seal.

## Rules of the world

- Dinosaurs are real animals with real limits: they tire, they bleed, they can be hurt by large zoo animals.
- Zoo animals are not helpless: an elephant matriarch is a six-ton animal that has defended a family for forty years.
- Cell service is dead from 1:53 p.m. because the site-seal switches on the exterior jammers. Keeper radios still work on the zoo-only channel (Bev and Mags talk in Chapter 3), so zoo staff know something is wrong but not what. Nobody can reach the outside world.
- The zoo gates stay locked by remote until the end of the book. The rail cut is the only way out.
- The feed tone sounds at 18:00. Dusk is at 6:10 p.m.
- All four free dinosaurs die. So does nearly every named person.

## Themes

- **Who decides acceptable risk, and who pays for it.**
- **Care as a form of courage:** keepers, siblings, and strangers choosing to protect others, often at a price.
- **Animals at home vs. animals displaced:** the zoo animals defend their homes while the dinosaurs are lost.
- **What we keep:** Imogen's choice between saving the last Jurassic animal and saving the living.
