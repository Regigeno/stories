# Story Bible: Feeding Hour

## Style guide

- **Voice:** Close third person, past tense. One point of view per chapter.
- **Tone:** Tense and grounded. Dread first, spectacle second. Short dry humor from people under stress is welcome, but never at the expense of the stakes.
- **Prose:** Concrete and physical. Name the sound, the smell, the weight. Avoid long stretches of explanation during action.
- **Dinosaurs as animals:** No monsters. They behave like animals: they are hungry, frightened, territorial, and confused by being in the wrong place and time. Theropods are feathered where the science supports it. No cinematic roaring on demand.
- **Animal chapters:** Close third on the animal, with no dialogue and no human thoughts. The animal perceives through scent, sound, vibration, and memory. No anthropomorphic speeches.
- **Chapter device:** Every chapter opens with an italic line giving the day and time ("*Saturday, 1:12 p.m.*"). The book runs from early afternoon to the following dawn.
- **Chapter length:** At least 2,000 words, usually 2,000 to 2,800.
- **Violence:** Graphic when it happens, never gratuitous. Consequences stay on the page.

## Setting

**Calder Bay Zoo** is a mid-sized zoo in the fictional port city of Calder Bay. It is built on a former rail yard and a hill of old quarry fill. A ring road of service tunnels, called **the Ring** by staff, runs under the whole site, linking the night houses of the exhibits. Zoo staff believe the Ring is for utilities and animal transfers.

**Zones:**

- **Main Gate and Carousel Court:** the central plaza, with the carousel, food court, and education center.
- **African Plains:** a large open yard with a dry moat, a lion kopje, and an elephant barn.
- **Okavango Lagoon:** a big water exhibit with hippos and Nile crocodiles, a boat ride, a pier, and a viewing deck.
- **Taiga Trail:** wooded Amur tiger exhibit with a glass tunnel that runs through it. The keepers' building is on the north side.
- **Arctic Coast:** polar bear exhibit with a deep pool and underwater viewing window.
- **Giraffe Overlook:** a tall feeding platform and a sprawling paddock next to the Plains.
- **The Reptile House:** closed for two years for "renovation". It sits above the lab's service lift.

## The lab

**Meridian Biologics** leases the sublevels from the zoo's board through a shell conservancy. Cover story: a "genetic rescue" program for endangered species. In truth, **Project Halcyon** has been reviving extinct dinosaurs from preserved proteins and genomic reconstruction.

- **Sublevel 1:** Reception, labs, and offices.
- **Sublevel 2:** Incubation and nursery.
- **Sublevel 3:** Large paddocks, cooling plant, and control room.
- **The Ring access:** Sealed hatches connect the paddocks to the zoo's Ring for moving animals to a loading dock. Several hatches were never fully blocked from the zoo side.
- **Sublevel 5:** Not on any floor plan the staff received. Its lift key is held by Warren Teague alone.

The program's stated mandate is the Cretaceous. "Cretaceous" spans about eighty million years, so the staff joke constantly that Halcyon is a zoo of animals that never met each other.

## The breach

1. A new rooftop cooling plant went online two weeks ago to impress an investor. It draws on the same power loop as paddock door controllers.
2. On the investor demonstration day (a Saturday, chosen because crowds help hide traffic), the cooling plant trips and sheds load. Door controllers revert to their **fail-open default** because Meridian's safety engineer was overruled when she asked for fail-closed.
3. Eleven animals get into the Ring. Several more leave the sublevels through hatches into the zoo's night houses.
4. Warren Teague orders a **site-seal**: the lab's exterior doors and the zoo gates' remote locks are closed "to contain the problem" and no alert goes to the zoo for 26 minutes, because an evacuation would end the program.

## The animals

### Zoo animals

- **Imani:** African elephant matriarch, 41, about 6 tons. Calm, protective, deeply bonded to Mags.
- **Zuberi:** Male African lion, 9. Scarred, tired, and vigilant.
- **Amara and Sabi:** Lionesses who do most of the hunting.
- **Kito:** Zuberi's 3-year-old son, impulsive.
- **Katya:** Amur tigress, 14. Cautious, deadly, and a mother (her cubs are 1 year old).
- **Misha and Dasha:** Katya's yearling cubs.
- **Gideon:** Bull hippo, 3 tons. Territorial and huge. The lagoon is his.
- **The crocodiles:** A group of eight Nile crocodiles, the largest called **Old Nile**.
- **Kofi:** Male reticulated giraffe, 5 years old, 2,800 lb. High-strung, kicks first.
- **Nanuq:** Male polar bear, 11, about 1,300 lb. Spends long days in the pool.

## People

### Priya Raman, 17
Senior in high school, aiming at veterinary school. Practical and careful, a planner who hates not having a plan. Took her brother to the zoo for his birthday with money from babysitting. Their mother works double shifts at the hospital. Priya carries the weight of being the "responsible one".

### Arjun Raman, 9
Obsessed with dinosaurs. Corrects people constantly ("birds *are* dinosaurs"). Brave in a way that is both a gift and a danger. Knows more about the animals than the adults think.

### Eddie Marsh, 26
Carnivore keeper on Taiga Trail. Two years on the job. Kind, nervous, overly competent in a crisis once he accepts it is one.

### Mags Delacroix, 58
Elephant keeper, 30 years at Calder Bay Zoo. Quiet, stubborn, funny in a dry way. Her husband died four years ago, and Imani is the closest thing she has to family. Has a bad knee.

### Dr. Tamsin Ode, 39
Zoo veterinarian. Fast, unsentimental, loyal to the animals. Carries a dart rifle.

### Theo Lindqvist, 34
Pilot of the Okavango boat ride. Ex-merchant mariner. Lost his license over a drinking incident and has been sober for three years. Quiet authority on the water.

### Rosalind Pike, 49
Off-duty paramedic on Theo's boat with her teenage daughter **Jess, 15**. Triage is her native language.

### Dr. Imogen Vale, 46
Project lead of Halcyon. Brilliant, tired, and complicit. She raised the safety flag and then signed off anyway. The book's moral center is her attempt to fix what she helped create.

### Warren Teague, 52
Vice president of operations at Meridian. Smooth, calm, and unwavering. He believes the project is worth any risk and sincerely thinks the crowd upstairs is manageable.

### Dale Calloway, 44
Meridian head of security. Ex-military. Follows orders until the orders are plainly wrong.

### Hugh Ashworth, 61
Zoo director. Knows nothing about the lab. Learns too late.

## Rules of the world

- Dinosaurs are real animals with real limits: they tire, they bleed, they can be hurt by large zoo animals.
- Zoo animals are not helpless: an elephant matriarch is a six-ton animal that has defended a family for forty years.
- The lab's sedative ("**Lullaby**", a long-acting tranquilizer designed for large theropods) works but needs time and volume.
- Cell and radio coverage on the site is poor, and the lab's jammer (installed to guard secrecy) is stuck on. Keeper radios work on a zoo-only channel.
- Dusk is at 6:10 p.m. The book covers about 17 hours.

## Themes

- **Who decides acceptable risk, and who pays for it.**
- **Care as a form of courage:** keepers, siblings, and strangers choosing to protect others.
- **Animals at home vs. animals displaced:** the zoo animals defend their homes while the dinosaurs are lost.
